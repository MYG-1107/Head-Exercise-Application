Cybersecurity Basics for Healthcare-Facing Web Apps

Healthcare-facing software may sit near sensitive records and important operational workflows. Security design should reflect the consequences of unauthorised access, alteration or downtime.

8 November 2026·General educational information

Protect identity and access

Use strong authentication for accounts that can access sensitive information. Apply role-based permissions and verify access on the server for every protected operation. Hiding a button in the interface is not access control. Remove credentials when roles change and review privileged access.

Protect data and system boundaries

Use encrypted transport, secure session handling and managed secrets. Separate development, test and production environments. Minimise real patient information in demonstrations and test datasets. Review suppliers that can host, process or access data, including their support and incident processes.

Plan continuity and evidence

Define recovery priorities, manual fallback processes and incident responsibilities. Test restoration rather than assuming backup jobs are sufficient. Healthcare obligations vary by location and product role; obtain qualified security, clinical and legal review before making compliance claims.

Key points

  • Enforce access on the server, not only in the interface.
  • Assess suppliers and integrations before production use.
  • Test backups, incident response and manual fallback.
Scope note

This article is for general education. It is not medical, clinical, legal, regulatory, cybersecurity incident-response or financial advice. Requirements depend on the specific product, people and jurisdiction. Seek qualified professional advice for high-impact decisions.
