Medical Software: Prototype vs Clinical Product

A working demonstration proves that code can execute a defined workflow. It does not establish clinical safety, diagnostic accuracy or regulatory status.

8 November 2026·General educational information

Start with the intended purpose

How a product is described and how people are expected to use it can affect its obligations. A utility that records user-entered values differs from software that analyses patient data to recommend diagnosis or treatment. Marketing should match actual intended use.

Understand what validation means

Software testing checks whether features behave as specified. Clinical validation asks different questions about performance in the intended context. Evidence may require representative data, documented protocols, risk management, usability evaluation and ongoing monitoring depending on product and jurisdiction.

Communicate limitations responsibly

A prototype should state what it does, what it does not do and what has not been tested. Avoid unsupported claims such as clinically proven or diagnostic. This article is general information, not medical, legal or regulatory advice; seek qualified assessment before clinical use.

Key points

  • Define intended use before writing health-related claims.
  • Separate software QA from clinical validation.
  • Seek jurisdiction-specific clinical and regulatory advice.
Scope note

This article is for general education. It is not medical, clinical, legal, regulatory, cybersecurity incident-response or financial advice. Requirements depend on the specific product, people and jurisdiction. Seek qualified professional advice for high-impact decisions.
