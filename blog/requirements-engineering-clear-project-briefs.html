Requirements Engineering: Turning a Business Idea into a Clear Project Brief

A project brief should reduce ambiguity. It helps clients, designers and developers agree what will be delivered, how it will be checked and which decisions remain open.

8 November 2026·General educational information

Describe roles and workflows

Identify people who use or are affected by the product. Describe the tasks they complete, information they need and exceptions that occur. Include hand-offs, approval points, notifications and external systems rather than listing screens alone.

Write testable acceptance criteria

A requirement such as “the app should be fast” is hard to verify. Define which page or action matters, under what conditions and what acceptable behaviour looks like. Separate functional requirements from performance, availability, accessibility and security needs.

Control change transparently

Prioritise requirements and agree how change requests are assessed. A new feature may affect integrations, testing, cost or launch dates. Record impact before approval and link the brief to test cases and release notes.

Key points

  • Map roles and workflows before listing screens.
  • Make acceptance criteria observable and testable.
  • Record assumptions, dependencies and change decisions.
Scope note

This article is for general education. It is not medical, clinical, legal, regulatory, cybersecurity incident-response or financial advice. Requirements depend on the specific product, people and jurisdiction. Seek qualified professional advice for high-impact decisions.
