Health Information Interoperability: The Basics for Product Teams

Interoperability is more than moving a file from one system to another. Receiving systems need to understand what the data means, where it came from and whether it is appropriate to use.

8 November 2026·General educational information

Think beyond the transport layer

A connection may succeed while the receiving application interprets a value incorrectly. Interoperability includes technical exchange, consistent structure, terminology, identity matching and governance. Map source systems, ownership, update frequency and correction workflows before design.

Understand standards in context

Standards such as HL7 FHIR provide structured ways to represent and exchange health information. Compatibility still depends on supported versions, profiles, terminology and implementation choices. Review the actual integration guide and test messages with the partner system.

Govern the exchange

Define who may request information, how identity is verified, how access is logged and what happens when systems disagree or a connection fails. Bring clinical, technical, privacy and operations stakeholders together before production exchange.

Key points

  • Confirm meaning and ownership, not only connectivity.
  • Validate the standards supported by both partners.
  • Agree access, audit, error and correction responsibilities.
Scope note

This article is for general education. It is not medical, clinical, legal, regulatory, cybersecurity incident-response or financial advice. Requirements depend on the specific product, people and jurisdiction. Seek qualified professional advice for high-impact decisions.
