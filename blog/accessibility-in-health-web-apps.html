Accessibility in Health-Related Web Applications

Accessibility is part of product quality. A health-related tool should not assume that everyone uses a mouse, sees colour clearly, hears audio or has the same device and network.

8 November 2026·General educational information

Make every action reachable

Use semantic buttons, links, headings, labels and form controls. A control that looks clickable but cannot receive keyboard focus creates a barrier. Keep focus visible and ensure focus order follows the natural task sequence. Icon-only controls need meaningful accessible names.

Make information perceivable

Use readable text, sufficient contrast and layouts that remain usable when zoomed. Never rely on colour alone to communicate status. Provide text alternatives for meaningful images and captions or transcripts for relevant audio and video. Write descriptive headings and link text.

Test with assistive technology

Support reduced-motion preferences, keyboard operation and touch interaction. Announce important status changes without interrupting users repeatedly. Use WCAG as a reference, then test complete workflows with people who use assistive technology; automated audits cannot prove full accessibility.

Key points

  • Use semantic HTML and visible keyboard focus.
  • Do not communicate meaning through colour alone.
  • Combine automated audits with human testing.
Scope note

This article is for general education. It is not medical, clinical, legal, regulatory, cybersecurity incident-response or financial advice. Requirements depend on the specific product, people and jurisdiction. Seek qualified professional advice for high-impact decisions.
