Data Minimisation and Retention: A Practical Governance Guide

Data governance becomes easier when teams know why each field exists, who uses it and when it should be removed. The objective is useful information with clear accountability.

8 November 2026·General educational information

Create a data inventory

List information collected by forms, APIs, integrations and background processes. Record purpose, source, storage location, access roles, sharing arrangements and retention. Include logs, backups and third-party services because information can spread beyond the primary database.

Set purpose and retention rules

Ask whether the product can complete the task without each field. Define retention by data type and purpose. Consider active records, backups, audit evidence and exports separately. Make deletion processes clear and understand when backup rotation may retain a protected recovery copy temporarily.

Make governance part of change control

New analytics, integrations and reporting features can change the data footprint. Include a data-flow review in release checklists and update notices and access rules when behaviour changes. Assign data owners and seek jurisdiction-specific advice for regulated or sensitive records.

Key points

  • Inventory data across the whole product.
  • Define purpose and retention for each category.
  • Review data flows whenever features or vendors change.
Scope note

This article is for general education. It is not medical, clinical, legal, regulatory, cybersecurity incident-response or financial advice. Requirements depend on the specific product, people and jurisdiction. Seek qualified professional advice for high-impact decisions.
